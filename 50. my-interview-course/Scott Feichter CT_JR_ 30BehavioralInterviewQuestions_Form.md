Interview prep 101 dictates that you should have your elevator pitch ready, a few stories polished, and a good sense of what you have to offer. So, how do you get there? Lots of practice, ideally aloud. 

To help you better prepare for your next interview, here are 30 behavioral interview questions sorted by topic that you can practice. 

Not sure how to answer these questions? [Here’s a quick guide on how to craft job-landing responses](https://www.themuse.com/advice/how-to-answer-tell-me-about-a-time-when-interview-questions). 

**TEAMWORK**

---

For questions like these, you want a story that illustrates your ability to work with others under challenging circumstances. Think team conflict, difficult project constraints, or clashing personalities. 

1. **Talk about a time when you had to work closely with someone whose personality was very different from yours.** 

When I worked in back office operations in the Finance Industry the Financial Advisor I reported to was quite different from me in his soceo-economic outlook and approach to customer service. 

However, I realized we each had different strengths that were valuable to completing the tasks necessary. I kept my mind open to be able to accept how our approaches could come together. 

Eventually we developed an excellent synergy based on mutual respect and hard work. We became a successful partnership, posting numbers for our office that far exceeded average. 

2. **Give me an example of a time you faced a conflict while working on a team. How did you handle that?** 

I received a small grant from the school I work for to develop a partnership among fellow faculty and bring to fruition a small project. 

During the course of this project there arose concerns about some overlap of the roles and responsibilities of the participants. 

I facilitated a meeting with the participants and created a safe space for open, honest communication. In this way we were better able to understand each others desires and capacities for the tasks at hand. 

Then I suggested a delegation of tasks with approximately equal qualitative and quantitative responsibility but that best matched the strengths and desires of the participants. 

I asked them for feedback about the delegation suggestions and with some small adjustments everyone felt we were proceeding in a fair and strategic manner.

Morale improved and the project was completed on time and underbudget, with excellent results that pleased the grant council. 

3. **Describe a time when you struggled to build a relationship with someone important. How did you eventually overcome that?** 

A colleague of mine had made assumptions about my background and upbringing which colored this person’s perception of my skills and credibility in the workplace. 

While these judgements were unfair and inaccurate, I remained patient with this person and exhibited grace and sympathy to their perspective. 

Eventually we learned more about each other and he began to respect me for my work and admire my patience and communication. 

Later he confessed to me that his initial judgement of me was a mistake \- not only because it was inaccurate \- but also because he learned that making such a judgement on inadequate information does not serve him well. 

He thanked me for my teamwork and presence in the workplace and expressed how happy he was to be working close with me on projects and I likewise reflected to him that I value his contributions greatly.  

4. **We all make mistakes we wish we could take back. Tell me about a time you wish you’d handled a situation differently with a colleague.**   
   

Once I had a colleague who seemed to be having a hard time. Though I noticed something was amiss I did not think to give it much consideration as I assumed it was an anomaly. 

Soon thereafter, this colleague tragically committed suicide. Though his scenario of trouble was not much related to the workplace I reflected back about this experience and realized I noticed things.

I came to understand that it is important to observe and consider flags and warning signs that our instincts point out to us. Taking time to slow down and reflect on what our intuition suggests is important. 

When this occurred in the future with another colleague I recognized it and took the opportunity to check in with them. 

As it turns out they were indeed struggling and we were able to help them get the appropriate resources and adjust in a healthy way. . 

5. **Tell me about a time you needed to get information from someone who wasn’t very responsive. What did you do?**

I needed to receive an authorization from a government agency to advance a learning project I was pursuing. I developed a relationship with a worker at the agency in pursuit of these tasks. 

Despite being careful to follow their preferred process of communication the information was not forthcoming in a manner acceptable for an upcoming deadline that all partiers were aware of. 

I used a strategy of patient persistence while also being mindful to respect people’s boundaries. I remained polite and professional while I reminded them in subsequent communications of the impending deadline. 

I put great effort to understand why I was not receiving the timely communication necessary and I kept a compassionate mindset of the challenges they face of having a high volume of requests. 

I  adjusted  the method of communication I was using \- since email was not yielding a response in an appropriate amount of time I contacted them via telephone.

Since my voice messages were also not yielding a reply in a reasonable time I visited the office in person, which was appropriate in this circumstance.

After politely explaininng the scenario to my contact’s co-workers I discovered that he had to take a sudden leave of absence due to illness of a family member. 

I was able to connect with a new contact there and received the information I needed in time for the pending deadline.  

**CLIENT-FACING SKILLS**

---

If the role you’re interviewing for works with clients, definitely be ready for one of these. Find an example of a time where you successfully represented your company or team and delivered exceptional customer service.

1. Describe a time when it was especially important to make a good impression on a client. How did you go about doing so?  
   

Any time meeting with a client, and especially the first time, it is critical to present oneself with professional and appropriate attire, hygiene, and preparation for tasks relevant to the encounter. 

When meeting new students, which is often,  I follow these basic guidelines mentioned above, as well as communicating to them in a manner that is welcoming and shows support for their goals in a positive environment free of judgements.  

2. Give me an example of a time when you did not meet a client’s expectation. What happened, and how did you attempt to rectify the situation?  
     
3. Tell me about a time when you made sure a customer was pleased with your service.  
     
4. Describe a time when you had to interact with a difficult client. What was the situation, and how did you handle it?  
     
5. When you’re working with a large number of customers, it’s tricky to deliver excellent service to them all. How do you go about prioritizing your customers’ needs?

**ABILITY TO ADAPT**

---

Times of turmoil are finally good for something\! Think of a recent work crisis you successfully navigated. Even if your navigation didn’t feel successful at the time, find a lesson or silver lining you took from the situation. 

1. Tell me about a time you were under a lot of pressure. What was going on, and how did you get through it?  
   

When the pandemic lockdowns were initiated we were challenged not only with the technological aspects of moving our learning sessions online, but also in how to compensate for things from the physical realm that cannot be duplicated in a digital environment. 

In realizing that though I cannot have as good a view of students physical technic and posture as in real life, nor demonstrate posture and technic as efficiently, there were some strengths to working via video that I could utilize.

For example, I can move the camera to an angle and closeness to my hands that far exceeds what is appropriate and possible in real life. 

2. Describe a time when your team or company was undergoing some change. How did that impact you, and how did you adapt?

When one of the schools I work for decided to undergo an expansion of the campus we were all required to adjust to the presence of construction on the premises. 

We took care to notify the student prior to this change and ensure that they would retain a level of confort and receive the proper level of service to facilitate their learning goals. 

For those who had concerns about potential noise we were able to reschedule them to times when we were assured the construction was not in progress. 

3. Tell me about the first job you’ve ever had. What did you do to learn the ropes?

The first job I ever had was delivering wedding cakes. I drove a pink van and wore a flashy uniform and it was difficult but fun\! 

To learn the ropes I observed co workers in action, paid close attention during training, and asked questions to my supervisor and co workers if I was not able to discover the correct solution myself in an reasonable effort. 

4. Give me an example of a time when you had to think on your feet in order to delicately extricate yourself from a difficult or awkward situation.  
     
5. Tell me about a time you failed. How did you deal with this situation?

**TIME MANAGEMENT SKILLS**

---

In other words, get ready to talk about a time you juggled multiple responsibilities, organized it all (perfectly), and completed everything before the deadline.

1. Tell me about a time you had to be very strategic in order to meet all your top priorities.  
     
2. Describe a long-term project that you managed. How did you keep everything moving along in a timely manner?  
     
3. Sometimes it’s just not possible to get everything on your to-do list done. Tell me about a time your responsibilities got a little overwhelming. What did you do?  
     
4. Tell me about a time you set a goal for yourself. How did you go about ensuring that you would meet your objective?  
     
5. Give me an example of a time you managed numerous responsibilities. How did you handle that?

**COMMUNICATION SKILLS**

---

You probably won’t have any trouble thinking of a story for communication questions, since it’s not only part of most jobs; it’s part of everyday life. However, the thing to remember here is to also talk about your thought process or preparation. 

1. Give me an example of a time when you were able to successfully persuade someone to see things your way at work.  
     
2. Describe a time when you were the resident technical expert. What did you do to make sure everyone was able to understand you?  
     
3. Tell me about a time when you had to rely on written communication to get your ideas across to your team.

4. Give me an example of a time when you had to explain something fairly complex to a frustrated client. How did you handle this delicate situation?  
     
5. Tell me about a successful presentation you gave and why you think it was a hit.

**MOTIVATION AND VALUE**  
---

A lot of [seemingly random questions](https://www.themuse.com/advice/4-steps-for-answering-offthewall-interview-questions) are actually attempting to learn more about what motivates you. Your response would ideally address this directly even if the question wasn’t explicit about it.

1. Tell me about your proudest professional accomplishment.  
     
2. Describe a time when you saw some problem and took the initiative to correct it rather than waiting for someone else to do it.  
     
3. Tell me about a time when you worked under close supervision or extremely loose supervision. How did you handle that?  
     
4. Give me an example of a time you were able to be creative with your work. What was exciting or difficult about it?  
     
5. Tell me about a time you were dissatisfied in your work. What could have been done to make it better?
