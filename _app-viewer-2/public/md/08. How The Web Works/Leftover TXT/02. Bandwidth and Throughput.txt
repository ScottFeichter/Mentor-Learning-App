Rate Of Data Transfer

The terms bandwidth and throughput are words used to describe the rate of data transfer.

In other words they describe aspects of the speed of transmitting and receiving of bits across a distance.



Bandwidth is the capacity of a medium to carry data.

Digital bandwidth is usually measured by the theoretical amount of bits that can be sent across the medium in a second, or bps.

bps - bits per second
kbps - thousand (Kilobit) bits per second
Mbps - million (Megabit) bits per second
Gbps - billion (Gigabit) bits per second
Tbps - trillion (Terabit) bits per second


Throughput is the actual measure of the transfer of bits across the medium over a given period of time.

However, due to a number of factors, throughput does not usually match the specified bandwidth.

Many factors influence throughput including:

- Amount of data being sent and received over the connection
- Types of data being transmitted
- Latency created by the number of network devices encountered between source and destination




NOTE: Bits are used to measure data speed whereas bytes are used to measure storage or file size. Therefore a gigabit and gigabyte are not the same amount and not used to measure the same concepts.
