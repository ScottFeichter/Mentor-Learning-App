# Works In Progress

## The Question

"So I think there are many people that would appreciate an uncorruptable browser that would not be subject to data tracking and advertisements. Could a new browser built with Rust and sandboxed with C++ provide this?"

## Privacy-Focused Browsers Already Exist

Several browsers prioritize privacy:

### Brave
- Built on Chromium (C++)
- Blocks ads and trackers by default
- Built-in Tor support
- Rewards users with cryptocurrency for viewing ads (optional)

### Firefox
- Written in C++ and Rust (Firefox is transitioning to Rust!)
- Enhanced Tracking Protection
- Open source, non-profit (Mozilla Foundation)
- Servo project: Experimental browser engine written entirely in Rust

### Safari
- **Built with**: C++ and Objective-C (WebKit engine)
- **Owned by**: Apple
- **Privacy stance**: Strong privacy features (Intelligent Tracking Prevention, Private Relay)
- **The catch**: Only on Apple devices (macOS, iOS)
- **Business model**: Apple makes money from hardware, not ads, so can afford privacy
- **Market share**: ~20% (mostly iOS users who have no choice)
- **Walled garden**: Tightly integrated with Apple ecosystem

Safari is interesting because Apple's business model (selling devices) aligns with privacy, unlike Google (selling ads). But it's locked to Apple hardware.

### Tor Browser
- Based on Firefox
- Routes traffic through Tor network
- Maximum anonymity

### LibreWolf
- Firefox fork
- Removes telemetry, ads, tracking

## Why They Haven't "Won"

### 1. The Network Effect Problem

Most people use Chrome because:
- It came pre-installed or was heavily promoted
- "Everyone else uses it"
- Works with all websites (compatibility)

Breaking this is nearly impossible, even with a better product.

### 2. The Money Problem

**Chrome/Edge**: Funded by Google/Microsoft (billions in revenue from ads/data)
- Thousands of engineers
- Marketing budgets
- Can afford to give it away free

**Safari**: Funded by Apple (billions in revenue from hardware)
- Large team
- But only for Apple devices

**Privacy browsers**: Limited funding
- Smaller teams
- Less marketing
- Harder to monetize (privacy = no ad revenue)

### 3. The Compatibility Problem

Websites are built and tested for Chrome. A new browser must:
- Support all existing web standards
- Render billions of existing websites correctly
- Keep up with constant web platform changes

This requires massive ongoing investment.

### 4. The Convenience Problem

Most users choose convenience over privacy:
- "I have nothing to hide"
- "I like personalized ads"
- "Syncing across devices is nice"

Privacy requires trade-offs (slower, less convenient, fewer features).

## Could Rust Help?

**Yes, for security:**
- Memory safety prevents many browser vulnerabilities
- Firefox is already rewriting components in Rust (Servo project)

**But it doesn't solve:**
- Tracking (that's a policy/feature choice, not a language choice)
- Ads (websites embed them; browser can only block)
- Network effects (people use what others use)
- Funding (privacy browsers still need money)

## The Real Barriers

### Technical: Solvable
- Build a fast, secure browser in Rust ✓ (possible)
- Block trackers and ads ✓ (already done)

### Non-Technical: Hard
- Get people to switch (network effects)
- Fund development without selling user data
- Maintain compatibility with the web
- Compete with billion-dollar companies

## What Would It Take?

**1. Massive Funding**
- Government support, or
- Billionaire benefactor, or
- Sustainable business model (hard without ads/data), or
- Hardware company (like Apple with Safari)

**2. Killer Feature**
- Something so compelling people switch despite inconvenience
- Privacy alone hasn't been enough

**3. Regulatory Change**
- Laws forcing browser choice (like EU did with default browsers)
- Breaking up big tech monopolies

## Browser Market Share (2025)

- **Chrome**: ~65% (Google - ad-funded)
- **Safari**: ~20% (Apple - hardware-funded, iOS only)
- **Edge**: ~5% (Microsoft - Chromium-based)
- **Firefox**: ~3% (Mozilla - donations/partnerships)
- **Brave/Others**: <2%

## The Optimistic View

**It's happening slowly:**
- Firefox uses Rust extensively
- Brave is growing
- Safari has strong privacy features (but locked to Apple)
- Privacy awareness is increasing
- Regulations (GDPR, CCPA) are forcing change

**But realistically:**
- Chrome dominates
- Safari is strong but Apple-only
- Most users don't care enough about privacy to switch
- The economic incentives favor surveillance capitalism

## The Bottom Line

A privacy-focused Rust browser is **technically feasible** (and partially exists), but **economically and socially challenging**.

The problem isn't "can we build it?" (yes), it's "can we get people to use it and fund it sustainably?" (much harder).

Safari shows that privacy is possible with the right business model (hardware sales), but requires being a trillion-dollar company with a device ecosystem.

Your instinct is right - there's demand for this. But demand alone doesn't overcome network effects and billion-dollar marketing budgets.
