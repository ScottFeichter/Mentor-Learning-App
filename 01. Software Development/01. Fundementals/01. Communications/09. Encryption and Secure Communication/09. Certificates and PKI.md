# Certificates and PKI

&nbsp;

A digital certificate is a file that binds a public key to an identity — proving that a public key belongs to who it claims to belong to.

Public Key Infrastructure (PKI) is the system of standards, policies, and technologies that manages the creation, distribution, and verification of digital certificates.

## The Problem Certificates Solve

Asymmetric encryption relies on public keys — but how do you know a public key actually belongs to who you think it does?

Without certificates, an attacker could substitute their own public key and intercept communications.

## Certificate Authorities (CAs)

A Certificate Authority is a trusted third party that issues and signs digital certificates.

When a CA signs a certificate, it is vouching that the public key in the certificate belongs to the stated entity.

Browsers and operating systems come with a list of trusted CAs built in.

## How It Works

1. A website generates a key pair and submits a Certificate Signing Request (CSR) to a CA
2. The CA verifies the identity of the requester
3. The CA issues a signed certificate containing the public key and identity information
4. When a browser connects to the site, it receives the certificate and verifies the CA's signature
5. If trusted, the browser uses the public key to establish a secure connection

## Certificate Contents
