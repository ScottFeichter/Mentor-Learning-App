# VPNs

&nbsp;

A VPN (Virtual Private Network) creates an encrypted tunnel between a device and a remote server, routing all traffic through that tunnel.

## What It Does

- Encrypts all traffic between the device and the VPN server
- Masks the device's real IP address — the destination sees the VPN server's IP instead
- Allows secure access to private networks over the public internet

## How It Works

The VPN client on the device encrypts outgoing traffic and sends it to the VPN server.

The VPN server decrypts the traffic and forwards it to the destination on behalf of the client.

Responses travel back through the same encrypted tunnel.

## Common Protocols

- <span class="olive-bold">OpenVPN</span> — open source, widely trusted
- <span class="olive-bold">WireGuard</span> — modern, fast, and simpler than OpenVPN
- <span class="olive-bold">IPSec</span> — often used in enterprise and site-to-site VPNs
- <span class="olive-bold">L2TP/IPSec</span> — older combination protocol

## Use Cases

- <span class="olive-bold">Remote work</span> — employees securely access company networks from home
- <span class="olive-bold">Privacy</span> — hiding browsing activity from ISPs or on public Wi-Fi
- <span class="olive-bold">Site-to-site</span> — connecting two office networks securely over the internet
- <span class="olive-bold">Bypassing geo-restrictions</span> — accessing content available in other regions

## Limitations

VPNs encrypt the tunnel but do not make you anonymous — the VPN provider can see your traffic.

They also add latency since all traffic is routed through an additional server.
