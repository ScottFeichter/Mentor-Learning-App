# History of Encryption

&nbsp;

Encryption is one of the oldest practices in human communication — the need to keep messages secret predates computers by thousands of years.

## Ancient Encryption

- <span class="olive-bold">Caesar Cipher</span> — used by Julius Caesar, shifts each letter by a fixed number of positions in the alphabet
- <span class="olive-bold">Scytale</span> — a Spartan device that wrapped a strip of parchment around a rod to encode messages

## The Enigma Machine

During World War II, Germany used the Enigma machine — an electromechanical cipher device that produced extremely complex substitution ciphers.

Breaking Enigma at Bletchley Park, largely through the work of Alan Turing, is considered one of the pivotal moments in the history of computing and cryptography.

## The Digital Era

With the rise of computers, encryption became mathematical rather than mechanical.

- <span class="olive-bold">1970s</span> — DES (Data Encryption Standard) published by NIST, first widely adopted digital encryption standard
- <span class="olive-bold">1976</span> — Diffie and Hellman publish the concept of public key cryptography
- <span class="olive-bold">1977</span> — RSA algorithm developed
- <span class="olive-bold">2001</span> — AES (Advanced Encryption Standard) replaces DES

## Modern Encryption

Today encryption is everywhere — HTTPS, messaging apps, file storage, and payment systems all rely on it.

The field continues to evolve as computing power increases and quantum computing threatens to break current standards.
