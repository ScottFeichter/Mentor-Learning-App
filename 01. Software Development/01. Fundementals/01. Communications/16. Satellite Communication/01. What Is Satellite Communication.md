# What Is Satellite Communication?

&nbsp;

Satellite communication is the use of artificial satellites orbiting the Earth to relay signals between locations on the ground.

A ground station transmits a signal up to a satellite, which amplifies and retransmits it back down to another location.

## How It Works

- A transmitting station sends a signal up to the satellite — called the uplink
- The satellite receives, amplifies, and retransmits the signal — called the transponder
- A receiving station picks up the signal — called the downlink

## What It Is Used For

- Television and radio broadcasting
- GPS and navigation
- Internet access in remote areas
- Weather monitoring
- Military communications
- Scientific research

## Advantages

- Can reach any location on Earth including oceans, deserts, and polar regions
- A single satellite can cover a vast geographic area

## Limitations

- High latency due to the distance signals must travel to orbit and back
- Expensive to launch and maintain
- Susceptible to weather interference
