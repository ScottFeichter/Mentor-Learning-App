# Transistors

&nbsp;

A transistor is a semiconductor device that can act as an electrically controlled switch or amplifier.

It is the fundamental building block of all modern digital electronics.

## How It Works

A transistor has three terminals — a source, a drain, and a gate.

A small voltage applied to the gate controls whether current can flow between the source and the drain.

This on/off behavior is what makes a transistor a switch — and a switch is all you need to represent a 1 or a 0.

## Why It Matters

Logic gates are built from transistors.

A single modern CPU contains billions of transistors switching on and off billions of times per second.

The miniaturization of transistors over decades — described by Moore's Law — is what made modern computing possible.

## From Vacuum Tubes To Transistors

Before transistors, computers used vacuum tubes to switch electrical signals.

Vacuum tubes were large, fragile, and generated enormous heat.

The invention of the transistor in 1947 replaced them with something small, reliable, and cheap to manufacture.
