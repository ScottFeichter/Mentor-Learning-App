# ARPANET

&nbsp;

ARPANET (Advanced Research Projects Agency Network) was the first packet-switched computer network and the direct predecessor of the internet.

## Background

In the 1960s, the US Department of Defense's Advanced Research Projects Agency (ARPA) funded research into a communication network that could survive a nuclear attack by routing around damaged nodes.

## Key Innovations

- <span class="olive-bold">Packet switching</span> — instead of dedicating a circuit between two points, data is broken into packets that can take different routes and be reassembled at the destination. This was a radical departure from the circuit-switched telephone model.
- <span class="olive-bold">Distributed architecture</span> — no single point of failure, the network could route around damage

## Timeline

- <span class="olive-bold">1969</span> — first ARPANET message sent between UCLA and Stanford Research Institute. The system crashed after the first two letters ("LO" of "LOGIN") — making "LO" the first message ever sent over a computer network.
- <span class="olive-bold">1971</span> — 15 nodes connected
- <span class="olive-bold">1972</span> — email introduced on ARPANET
- <span class="olive-bold">1983</span> — ARPANET adopts TCP/IP, the moment considered the birth of the internet

## Legacy

ARPANET proved that packet switching worked at scale and that a distributed network of computers could communicate reliably.

Every concept it pioneered — packets, routing, distributed architecture — is still at the core of the internet today.
