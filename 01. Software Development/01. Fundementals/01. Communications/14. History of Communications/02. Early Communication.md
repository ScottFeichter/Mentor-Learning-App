# Early Communication

&nbsp;

Before writing, all human communication was spoken — limited to the range of a voice and the memory of a listener.

## Spoken Language

Spoken language is the oldest form of human communication.

It allowed complex ideas to be shared but was limited to those physically present.

## Visual Signals

Early civilizations developed visual signaling systems to communicate over distance:

- <span class="olive-bold">Smoke signals</span> — used by many cultures to send simple messages across distances
- <span class="olive-bold">Beacon fires</span> — chains of fires used to relay alerts across large distances quickly
- <span class="olive-bold">Semaphore</span> — a system of flags or mechanical arms on towers used to relay messages visually, developed in the 18th century

## Writing

Writing was one of the most transformative inventions in human history.

It allowed information to be recorded, stored, and transported — decoupling communication from the presence of the speaker.

Early writing systems include cuneiform (Mesopotamia, ~3200 BCE) and hieroglyphics (Egypt, ~3100 BCE).

## The Printing Press

Gutenberg's printing press (~1440) made the mass reproduction of written material possible for the first time.

It dramatically reduced the cost of books, accelerated the spread of knowledge, and is considered one of the most significant inventions in history.
