# Advantages and Limitations

&nbsp;

Fiber optic communication has significant advantages over copper-based systems but also comes with real limitations.

## Advantages

- <span class="olive-bold">Extremely high bandwidth</span> — capable of carrying terabits per second
- <span class="olive-bold">Low signal loss</span> — signals travel hundreds of kilometers without significant degradation
- <span class="olive-bold">Immune to EMI</span> — light is not affected by electromagnetic interference
- <span class="olive-bold">No crosstalk</span> — signals in adjacent fibers do not interfere with each other
- <span class="olive-bold">Secure</span> — fiber does not radiate signals, making it difficult to tap without detection
- <span class="olive-bold">Lightweight</span> — fiber cables are lighter and thinner than equivalent copper cables

## Limitations

- <span class="olive-bold">Cost</span> — fiber is more expensive to install than copper, particularly for last mile connections
- <span class="olive-bold">Fragility</span> — glass fibers can break if bent too sharply or subjected to physical stress
- <span class="olive-bold">Specialized equipment</span> — requires optical transceivers and splicing equipment that copper does not
- <span class="olive-bold">One way per fiber</span> — each fiber carries light in one direction, so bidirectional communication requires either two fibers or wavelength division multiplexing
