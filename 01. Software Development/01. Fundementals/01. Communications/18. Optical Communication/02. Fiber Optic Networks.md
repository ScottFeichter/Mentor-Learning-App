# Fiber Optic Networks

&nbsp;

Fiber optic networks use fiber optic cables to transmit data as light pulses across local, regional, and global distances.

## Structure

A fiber optic cable contains one or more thin strands of glass or plastic called optical fibers.

Each fiber is surrounded by a cladding layer that reflects light back into the core, keeping the signal contained as it travels.

Multiple fibers are bundled together and protected by an outer jacket.

## Types of Fiber

- <span class="olive-bold">Single-mode fiber</span> — very thin core, carries a single beam of light, used for long distance transmission
- <span class="olive-bold">Multi-mode fiber</span> — wider core, carries multiple beams, used for shorter distances within buildings and data centers

## Network Tiers

- <span class="olive-bold">Backbone / Long haul</span> — undersea and intercontinental cables connecting countries and continents
- <span class="olive-bold">Metro networks</span> — connecting cities and regions
- <span class="olive-bold">Last mile</span> — fiber to the home or business (FTTH/FTTB)

## Speeds

Modern fiber optic cables can carry terabits of data per second.

A single fiber strand can carry multiple wavelengths of light simultaneously using wavelength division multiplexing (WDM), multiplying capacity further.
