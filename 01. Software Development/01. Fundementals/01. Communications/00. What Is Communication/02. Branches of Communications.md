# The Branches of Communications

&nbsp;

Communications is a broad field.

It encompasses every way that information is created, transmitted, and received — between people, machines, and organizations.

## The Six Branches

<div class="indent">

### Technical and Electronic Communications
The foundation of the modern digital world.

Concerned with how machines encode, transmit, and process information.

Gave rise to Computer Science, networking, and the internet.

### Telecommunications
The infrastructure layer.

Concerned with transmitting information over distances — telephone, radio, satellite, fiber, and wireless systems.

Closely related to Technical Communications but focused on the physical and systemic infrastructure of transmission.

### Mass Communications
The broadcast layer.

Concerned with reaching large audiences through media — journalism, broadcasting, publishing, advertising, and digital media.

Depends heavily on both Telecommunications and Technical Communications to distribute content at scale.

### Visual Communications
The design layer.

Concerned with conveying meaning through visual elements — design, symbols, imagery, and interface.

Overlaps with Mass Communications in media and with Technical Communications in UI and data visualization.

### Organizational Communications
The institutional layer.

Concerned with how information flows within and between organizations — leadership, culture, knowledge, and crisis.

Draws on Interpersonal Communications at the human level and Technical Communications at the systems level.

### Interpersonal Communications
The human layer.

Concerned with direct exchange between individuals — language, rhetoric, psychology, and culture.

The most fundamental branch — all other branches ultimately serve or depend on human communication.

</div>

## How They Relate

Each branch is distinct but none operates in isolation.

Technical Communications builds the systems that Mass Communications uses to reach audiences.

Telecommunications provides the infrastructure that Technical Communications runs on.

Visual Communications shapes how information is presented across all other branches.

Organizational Communications governs how institutions use all of the above.

Interpersonal Communications underlies every exchange at the human level, regardless of medium.
