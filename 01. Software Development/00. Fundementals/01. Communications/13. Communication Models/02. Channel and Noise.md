# Channel and Noise

&nbsp;

The channel is the medium through which a message travels from sender to receiver.

Noise is anything that interferes with the message during transmission.

## The Channel

A channel can be physical or logical.

- <span class="olive-bold">Physical channels</span> — copper wire, fiber optic cable, air (for wireless), light
- <span class="olive-bold">Logical channels</span> — a TCP connection, an HTTP request, a frequency band

The properties of the channel — bandwidth, latency, reliability — determine how well a message can be transmitted.

## Noise in the Channel

Noise is any unwanted addition to the signal that was not part of the original message.

In technical systems noise can be:

- Electromagnetic interference
- Signal attenuation over distance
- Packet loss in a network
- Bit errors caused by hardware faults

## Shannon's Channel Capacity

Claude Shannon proved mathematically that every channel has a maximum rate at which information can be transmitted reliably given a certain level of noise.

This is known as the Shannon Limit and is foundational to information theory and modern communications engineering.
