# How Data Travels

&nbsp;

Data on the internet does not travel as a single continuous stream — it is broken into small chunks called packets and reassembled at the destination.

## Packet Switching

The internet uses packet switching to transmit data.

Each packet contains a portion of the data along with a header that includes the source address, destination address, and sequence number.

Packets from the same transmission can take different routes through the network and arrive out of order — the destination reassembles them in the correct sequence.

## The Journey of a Packet

1. Data is broken into packets by the sender
2. Each packet is labeled with source and destination IP addresses
3. The packet is sent to the nearest router
4. Each router reads the destination address and forwards the packet to the next router on the best available path
5. The packet hops across multiple networks and routers until it reaches the destination
6. The destination reassembles all packets into the original data

## Protocols Involved

- <span class="olive-bold">IP (Internet Protocol)</span> — handles addressing and routing
- <span class="olive-bold">TCP (Transmission Control Protocol)</span> — ensures all packets arrive and are reassembled correctly
- <span class="olive-bold">UDP (User Datagram Protocol)</span> — faster but does not guarantee delivery, used for video and gaming

## Physical Path

Packets travel through a combination of fiber optic cables, copper wires, wireless links, and satellite connections depending on the route.
