# Latency

&nbsp;

Latency is the time it takes for a signal or packet of data to travel from its source to its destination.

It is measured in milliseconds (ms) and is often referred to as delay.

## Sources of Latency

- <span class="olive-bold">Propagation delay</span> — the time it takes a signal to physically travel through the medium
- <span class="olive-bold">Transmission delay</span> — the time it takes to push all the bits of a packet onto the wire
- <span class="olive-bold">Processing delay</span> — the time routers and switches take to process and forward packets
- <span class="olive-bold">Queuing delay</span> — the time a packet spends waiting in a buffer before being processed

## Latency vs Bandwidth

High bandwidth does not mean low latency.

You can have a very wide pipe that still takes a long time to deliver data if the distance is large.

A satellite connection may have high bandwidth but very high latency due to the distance the signal must travel.

## Why It Matters

Latency is critical for real time applications — video calls, online gaming, and live streaming are all sensitive to high latency even when bandwidth is sufficient.
