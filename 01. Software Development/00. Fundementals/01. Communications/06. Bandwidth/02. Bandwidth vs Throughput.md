# Bandwidth vs Throughput

&nbsp;

Bandwidth and throughput are related but distinct concepts.

## Bandwidth

Bandwidth is the theoretical maximum capacity of a channel — how much data it could carry under ideal conditions.

It is a property of the channel itself.

## Throughput

Throughput is the actual amount of data successfully transmitted over a channel in a given time period.

It is always less than or equal to bandwidth due to real world factors like noise, congestion, protocol overhead, and packet loss.

## Analogy

A highway has a maximum capacity — that is bandwidth.

The number of cars actually moving at any given moment — that is throughput.

Traffic jams, accidents, and speed limits all reduce throughput below the theoretical maximum.

## Why The Distinction Matters

An internet provider may advertise 1 Gbps bandwidth but your actual throughput will be lower depending on network conditions, hardware, and distance from the server.
