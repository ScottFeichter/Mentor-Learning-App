# Conversion

&nbsp;

Conversion is the process of translating a signal between analog and digital forms.

Since the real world is analog and computers are digital, conversion happens constantly in everyday technology.

## Analog to Digital (ADC)

An Analog to Digital Converter samples an analog signal at regular intervals and assigns each sample a discrete binary value.

The quality of the conversion depends on two factors:

- <span class="olive-bold">Sample Rate</span> — how many times per second the signal is measured <span class="muted">(higher = more accurate)</span>
- <span class="olive-bold">Bit Depth</span> — how many binary values are available to represent each sample <span class="muted">(higher = more precision)</span>

## Digital to Analog (DAC)

A Digital to Analog Converter takes a sequence of binary values and reconstructs a continuous analog signal from them.

This is what happens when your computer plays audio through speakers — the digital audio file is converted back into an analog electrical signal that drives the speaker cone.

## Examples

- A microphone converting sound waves into a digital audio file <span class="muted">(ADC)</span>
- A speaker playing back a digital audio file <span class="muted">(DAC)</span>
- A digital camera converting light into pixel values <span class="muted">(ADC)</span>
- A screen displaying those pixel values as light <span class="muted">(DAC)</span>
