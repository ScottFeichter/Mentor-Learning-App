# AM and FM

&nbsp;

AM and FM are two methods of modulating a radio carrier wave to encode audio information for broadcast.

## AM (Amplitude Modulation)

AM encodes the audio signal by varying the amplitude (height) of the carrier wave.

- Frequencies: 535 kHz to 1,705 kHz
- Travels farther than FM, especially at night when signals can bounce off the ionosphere
- More susceptible to noise and interference
- Lower audio quality than FM
- Used for talk radio, news, and long distance broadcasting

## FM (Frequency Modulation)

FM encodes the audio signal by varying the frequency of the carrier wave.

- Frequencies: 87.5 MHz to 108 MHz
- Less susceptible to noise and interference than AM
- Higher audio quality — supports stereo sound
- Shorter range than AM
- Used for music broadcasting and higher fidelity audio

## Why The Difference Matters

AM's longer range made it dominant in early radio broadcasting.

FM's better sound quality made it the preferred format for music once the technology matured.

Both remain in use today, though digital broadcasting is gradually supplementing both.
