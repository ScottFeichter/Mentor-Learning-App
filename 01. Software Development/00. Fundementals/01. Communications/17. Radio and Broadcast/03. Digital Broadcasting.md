# Digital Broadcasting

&nbsp;

Digital broadcasting is the transmission of audio and video content encoded as digital data rather than as analog signals.

## How It Differs From Analog

Analog broadcasting transmits a continuously varying signal.

Digital broadcasting transmits binary data — the content is encoded as a stream of 0s and 1s.

The receiver decodes the digital stream back into audio and video.

## Advantages Over Analog

- <span class="olive-bold">Better quality</span> — digital signals do not degrade gradually like analog; they either work or they don't
- <span class="olive-bold">More efficient</span> — more channels can fit in the same bandwidth
- <span class="olive-bold">Additional data</span> — digital broadcasts can carry program guides, subtitles, and interactive content alongside audio and video
- <span class="olive-bold">Error correction</span> — digital systems can detect and correct transmission errors

## Standards

- <span class="olive-bold">DAB (Digital Audio Broadcasting)</span> — digital radio standard used in Europe and Australia
- <span class="olive-bold">HD Radio</span> — digital radio standard used in the United States
- <span class="olive-bold">DVB (Digital Video Broadcasting)</span> — digital television standard used in most of the world
- <span class="olive-bold">ATSC</span> — digital television standard used in North America

## The Analog Switchoff

Most countries have completed or are completing a transition from analog to digital broadcasting, freeing up spectrum for other uses like mobile networks.
