# Mobile Networks

&nbsp;

Mobile networks are wireless telecommunications networks that allow devices to communicate while moving.

They are built on a system of geographic cells, each served by a base station — which is why they are also called cellular networks.

## How They Work

A geographic area is divided into cells.

Each cell has a base station (cell tower) that communicates wirelessly with devices in its area.

As a device moves between cells, the connection is handed off from one base station to the next seamlessly.

## Generations

- <span class="olive-bold">1G</span> — analog voice only <span class="muted">(1980s)</span>
- <span class="olive-bold">2G</span> — digital voice and SMS <span class="muted">(1990s)</span>
- <span class="olive-bold">3G</span> — mobile internet, video calls <span class="muted">(2000s)</span>
- <span class="olive-bold">4G LTE</span> — high speed mobile broadband <span class="muted">(2010s)</span>
- <span class="olive-bold">5G</span> — very high speed, low latency, supports massive device density <span class="muted">(2020s)</span>

## Why It Matters

Mobile networks are how the majority of the world accesses the internet today.

They are also the backbone of IoT — connecting billions of devices beyond phones and computers.
