# Telephone Networks

&nbsp;

Telephone networks are the infrastructure that carries voice communication between parties over distance.

## The Public Switched Telephone Network (PSTN)

The PSTN is the traditional global telephone network — the collection of interconnected circuit-switched telephone lines, cables, satellites, and switching centers that has existed since the late 19th century.

When you make a call, the PSTN establishes a dedicated circuit between the two parties for the duration of the call.

## Circuit Switching

Traditional telephone networks use circuit switching — a dedicated path is reserved between caller and receiver for the entire call.

This guarantees consistent quality but is inefficient since the circuit is held open even during silence.

## Voice Over IP (VoIP)

Modern telephone systems increasingly use VoIP — transmitting voice as digital packets over the internet rather than over dedicated circuits.

- <span class="olive-bold">Examples</span> — Skype, WhatsApp calls, Zoom, business phone systems
- More efficient than circuit switching
- Quality depends on internet connection

## The Transition

The PSTN is gradually being replaced by digital and IP-based systems worldwide.
