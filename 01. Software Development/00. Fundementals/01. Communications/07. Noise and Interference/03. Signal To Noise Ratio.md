# Signal To Noise Ratio

&nbsp;

Signal to Noise Ratio (SNR) is a measure of the strength of a desired signal relative to the level of background noise.

It is expressed in decibels (dB) — a higher SNR means a cleaner, more reliable signal.

## How It Works

SNR = Signal Power / Noise Power

A high SNR means the signal is much stronger than the noise — the information is easy to distinguish.

A low SNR means the noise is close to or overwhelming the signal — the information becomes difficult or impossible to recover.

## Why It Matters

SNR directly affects the quality and reliability of a communication channel.

- In audio — a low SNR produces hiss and static
- In networking — a low SNR increases bit error rates and reduces throughput
- In wireless — a low SNR causes dropped connections and slower speeds

## Improving SNR

- Increase transmitter power
- Use shielded or higher quality cables
- Reduce the distance between transmitter and receiver
- Use error correction to compensate for noise at the digital level
