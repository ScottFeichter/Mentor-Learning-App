# Fiber Optics

&nbsp;

Fiber optic cable transmits data as pulses of light through thin strands of glass or plastic rather than as electrical signals through metal.

## How It Works

A laser or LED converts an electrical signal into light pulses.

Those pulses travel through the fiber core using total internal reflection — the light bounces along the inside of the fiber without escaping.

A photodetector at the other end converts the light back into an electrical signal.

## Advantages

- Extremely high bandwidth — capable of terabits per second
- Very low signal loss — signals travel hundreds of kilometers without amplification
- Immune to electromagnetic interference
- Lightweight and thin compared to copper cables

## Limitations

- More expensive to install than copper
- Requires specialized equipment for splicing and termination
- Glass fibers are fragile and can break if bent too sharply

## Common Uses

- Internet backbone and undersea cables
- Data center interconnects
- Fiber to the home (FTTH) broadband
- Long distance telecommunications
