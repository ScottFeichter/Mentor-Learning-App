# Wireless

&nbsp;

Wireless transmission uses electromagnetic waves to carry data through the air without a physical medium.

It is unguided media — the signal radiates outward from the transmitter rather than traveling along a defined path.

## How It Works

A transmitter converts data into electromagnetic waves and broadcasts them via an antenna.

A receiver picks up those waves and converts them back into data.

The frequency of the waves determines the range, speed, and penetration characteristics of the signal.

## Common Wireless Technologies

- <span class="olive-bold">Wi-Fi</span> — local area wireless networking, typically 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz
- <span class="olive-bold">Bluetooth</span> — short range device to device communication
- <span class="olive-bold">Cellular (4G/5G)</span> — wide area mobile networking via cell towers
- <span class="olive-bold">Satellite</span> — very long range via orbiting satellites
- <span class="olive-bold">Infrared</span> — very short range, line of sight <span class="muted">(TV remotes)</span>

## Advantages

- No physical cables required
- Supports mobility — devices can move while staying connected
- Easy to deploy in areas where cabling is impractical

## Limitations

- Susceptible to interference from other devices and physical obstacles
- Generally lower bandwidth and higher latency than wired equivalents
- Security requires additional measures since signals can be intercepted
