# Comparing Transmission Media

&nbsp;

Each transmission medium has different characteristics that make it suited to different use cases.

## Comparison

| Medium | Bandwidth | Distance | Interference | Cost | Mobility |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Twisted Pair (Copper) | Medium | Short-Medium | Moderate | Low | No |
| Coaxial Cable | Medium-High | Medium | Low | Medium | No |
| Fiber Optics | Very High | Very Long | None | High | No |
| Wireless | Medium | Varies | High | Low-Medium | Yes |

## Key Takeaways

- <span class="olive-bold">Fiber</span> wins on bandwidth, distance, and interference resistance — but costs more to install
- <span class="olive-bold">Copper</span> is cheap and sufficient for most short distance needs
- <span class="olive-bold">Coaxial</span> sits between copper and fiber — better shielding than twisted pair but largely being replaced
- <span class="olive-bold">Wireless</span> is the only option that supports mobility — the tradeoff is susceptibility to interference and lower reliability

## Trends

Fiber is steadily replacing copper for backbone and long distance links.

Wireless is expanding as 5G and Wi-Fi 6 push speeds and reliability closer to wired equivalents.

Copper remains dominant for the last mile in many areas due to the cost of replacing existing infrastructure.
