# TCP/IP and The Internet

&nbsp;

TCP/IP is the suite of protocols that defines how data is transmitted across the internet.

Its adoption in 1983 is considered the birth of the modern internet.

## The Problem It Solved

ARPANET connected computers but different networks used incompatible protocols.

There was no standard way for a computer on one network to communicate with a computer on a different network.

## Vint Cerf and Bob Kahn

In 1974, Vint Cerf and Bob Kahn published "A Protocol for Packet Network Intercommunication" — the paper that introduced TCP.

They are widely credited as the co-inventors of the internet.

## TCP/IP

The protocol suite has two core components:

- <span class="olive-bold">IP (Internet Protocol)</span> — handles addressing and routing, getting packets from source to destination
- <span class="olive-bold">TCP (Transmission Control Protocol)</span> — handles reliable delivery, ensuring packets arrive complete and in order

## January 1, 1983 — Flag Day

On January 1, 1983, ARPANET switched from its previous NCP protocol to TCP/IP.

All connected computers had to make the switch simultaneously — this day is known as Flag Day and is considered the birth of the internet.

## Impact

TCP/IP provided a universal language for all networks.

Any network that adopted TCP/IP could communicate with any other — creating the conditions for the internet to grow from a research network into a global infrastructure.
