# The Telegraph

&nbsp;

The electric telegraph, developed in the 1830s and 1840s, was the first technology to transmit information near-instantly over long distances using electrical signals.

## How It Worked

A telegraph operator tapped a key to send pulses of electrical current along a wire.

The pulses were encoded using Morse code — a system of dots and dashes representing letters and numbers developed by Samuel Morse.

A receiver at the other end converted the pulses back into audible clicks or marks on paper tape.

## Key Figures

- <span class="olive-bold">Samuel Morse</span> — developed Morse code and co-invented the telegraph in the United States
- <span class="olive-bold">Charles Wheatstone and William Cooke</span> — developed the telegraph independently in Britain

## Impact

- Messages that once took days or weeks to deliver by post could now arrive in minutes
- Transformed commerce, journalism, and military coordination
- The first transatlantic telegraph cable was laid in 1858, connecting Europe and North America

## Why It Matters

The telegraph established the concept of electronic communication — encoding information as electrical signals and transmitting them over wires.

Every subsequent electronic communication technology, from the telephone to the internet, builds on this foundation.
