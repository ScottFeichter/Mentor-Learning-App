# The Telephone

&nbsp;

The telephone, patented by Alexander Graham Bell in 1876, was the first technology to transmit the human voice electrically over a wire.

## How It Worked

A microphone converted sound waves into a continuously varying electrical signal.

That signal traveled along a wire to a receiver, which converted it back into sound.

Unlike the telegraph which sent discrete coded pulses, the telephone transmitted analog audio — a continuous representation of the voice.

## The Patent Race

Bell filed his patent on February 14, 1876 — the same day as Elisha Gray filed a caveat for a similar device.

Bell was awarded the patent, one of the most valuable in history, though the priority dispute continued for years.

## The Public Switched Telephone Network (PSTN)

As telephone adoption grew, exchanges were built to connect callers — initially by human operators who manually connected calls.

Over decades this evolved into the global PSTN — the network of telephone lines, cables, and switching equipment that carried voice calls worldwide for over a century.

## Impact

- Transformed personal and business communication
- Created the infrastructure that would later carry data and internet traffic
- Established the model of point-to-point two-way communication

## Legacy

The telephone network's physical infrastructure — copper wires running to homes and businesses — became the first medium for internet access via dial-up and DSL.
