# Satellites

&nbsp;

Communication satellites extended the reach of telecommunications to every point on Earth — including oceans, polar regions, and areas with no ground-based infrastructure.

## The Space Age Begins

- <span class="olive-bold">1957</span> — Sputnik 1, the first artificial satellite, launched by the Soviet Union
- <span class="olive-bold">1962</span> — Telstar 1, the first active communications satellite, relays the first live transatlantic television signal
- <span class="olive-bold">1965</span> — Intelsat I (Early Bird), the first commercial geostationary communications satellite

## How Communication Satellites Work

A ground station transmits a signal up to the satellite (uplink).

The satellite amplifies and retransmits the signal back down to another location (downlink).

Geostationary satellites orbit at 35,786 km — high enough to appear stationary over a fixed point on Earth, allowing a single satellite to cover a third of the planet.

## Impact

- Enabled live global television broadcasting
- Provided telephone and data links to remote areas
- GPS navigation became possible through satellite constellations
- Weather forecasting transformed by continuous satellite imagery

## Modern Developments

Low Earth Orbit (LEO) constellations like Starlink are deploying thousands of satellites to provide low-latency broadband internet globally — a significant shift from the high-latency geostationary model.
