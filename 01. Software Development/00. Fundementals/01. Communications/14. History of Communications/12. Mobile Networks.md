# Mobile Networks

&nbsp;

Mobile networks brought internet connectivity to handheld devices — making the internet available anywhere, not just at a desk.

## The Generations

- <span class="olive-bold">1G (1980s)</span> — analog voice only, the first cellular networks
- <span class="olive-bold">2G (1990s)</span> — digital voice and SMS, introduced text messaging
- <span class="olive-bold">3G (2000s)</span> — mobile internet, video calls, enabled the first smartphones to browse the web
- <span class="olive-bold">4G LTE (2010s)</span> — high speed mobile broadband, made streaming and app-based services practical
- <span class="olive-bold">5G (2020s)</span> — very high speeds, very low latency, designed to support massive numbers of connected devices

## The Smartphone Era

The launch of the iPhone in 2007 and Android in 2008 combined mobile networks with powerful handheld computers.

The smartphone became the primary way most people access the internet worldwide.

## Impact

- More people access the internet via mobile than via desktop globally
- Mobile networks enabled entirely new categories of applications — ride sharing, food delivery, social media, mobile payments
- Connected billions of people in developing countries who had no access to fixed-line infrastructure

## IoT

5G is designed not just for phones but for the Internet of Things — billions of sensors, vehicles, and devices communicating over mobile networks.
