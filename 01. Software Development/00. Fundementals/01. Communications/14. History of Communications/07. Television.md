# Television

&nbsp;

Television extended radio broadcasting to include moving images — combining audio and video into a single transmitted signal.

## Development

- <span class="olive-bold">1884</span> — Paul Nipkow patents the Nipkow disk, an early mechanical scanning device
- <span class="olive-bold">1926</span> — John Logie Baird demonstrates the first working television system
- <span class="olive-bold">1936</span> — BBC launches the world's first regular public television service
- <span class="olive-bold">1940s-50s</span> — television rapidly becomes a mass medium in the United States and Europe

## How It Worked

A camera scanned a scene line by line, converting light into electrical signals.

Those signals were broadcast as radio waves.

A television receiver decoded the signal and used an electron beam to reproduce the image line by line on a phosphor screen.

## Color Television

Color TV was introduced in the 1950s, encoding color information alongside the existing black and white signal for backward compatibility.

## Impact

- Became the dominant mass medium of the 20th century
- Transformed politics, culture, advertising, and journalism
- The first live global broadcast — the Apollo 11 moon landing in 1969 — was watched by an estimated 600 million people

## The Transition to Digital

Analog television broadcasting has been replaced by digital broadcasting in most countries, freeing up spectrum and enabling higher definition and more channels.
