# The Modern Internet

&nbsp;

The modern internet is a global system of interconnected networks carrying an almost incomprehensible volume of data — and it continues to evolve rapidly.

## Scale

- Over 5 billion internet users worldwide
- Hundreds of billions of connected devices
- Petabytes of data transferred every day
- Undersea cables spanning every ocean connecting every continent

## The Infrastructure

The internet's physical backbone is a global network of:

- <span class="olive-bold">Undersea fiber optic cables</span> — carrying the vast majority of international internet traffic
- <span class="olive-bold">Internet Exchange Points (IXPs)</span> — physical locations where networks interconnect and exchange traffic
- <span class="olive-bold">Data centers</span> — housing the servers that store and serve content
- <span class="olive-bold">Content Delivery Networks (CDNs)</span> — distributing content closer to users to reduce latency

## Cloud Computing

The rise of cloud computing — AWS, Google Cloud, Azure — has centralized much of the internet's computing infrastructure into massive data centers operated by a handful of companies.

## Current Challenges

- <span class="olive-bold">IPv4 exhaustion</span> — the transition to IPv6 is ongoing
- <span class="olive-bold">Security</span> — cyberattacks, surveillance, and data breaches at unprecedented scale
- <span class="olive-bold">Centralization</span> — a small number of platforms control a large share of internet traffic
- <span class="olive-bold">Access</span> — billions of people still lack reliable internet access

## What Comes Next

Quantum networking, satellite constellations, and AI-driven infrastructure are among the technologies shaping the next era of communications.
