# Orbits

&nbsp;

The orbit a satellite occupies determines its coverage area, latency, and use case.

## Geostationary Orbit (GEO)

- Altitude of approximately 35,786 km
- Orbits at the same speed as Earth's rotation — appears stationary from the ground
- One satellite can cover about one third of the Earth's surface
- High latency (~600ms round trip) due to distance
- Used for TV broadcasting, weather satellites, and traditional internet services

## Medium Earth Orbit (MEO)

- Altitude between 2,000 and 35,786 km
- Lower latency than GEO
- Used for GPS and navigation satellites

## Low Earth Orbit (LEO)

- Altitude between 160 and 2,000 km
- Much lower latency than GEO (~20-40ms)
- Each satellite covers a smaller area so large constellations of hundreds or thousands of satellites are needed for global coverage
- Used by modern satellite internet services like Starlink

## Why Orbit Matters

The choice of orbit is a tradeoff between coverage, latency, and the number of satellites required.

LEO constellations are making satellite internet competitive with ground-based broadband for the first time.
