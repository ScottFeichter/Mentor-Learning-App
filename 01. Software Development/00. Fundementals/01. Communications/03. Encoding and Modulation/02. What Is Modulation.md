# What Is Modulation?

&nbsp;

Modulation is the process of varying a carrier signal in order to encode information for transmission.

A carrier signal is a steady wave — modulation shapes that wave to carry data.

## How It Works

A carrier wave has three properties that can be varied to encode information:

- <span class="olive-bold">Amplitude</span> — the height of the wave
- <span class="olive-bold">Frequency</span> — how many cycles per second
- <span class="olive-bold">Phase</span> — the timing offset of the wave

By varying one or more of these properties in a controlled way, information is embedded into the carrier.

## Common Types

- <span class="olive-bold">AM (Amplitude Modulation)</span> — varies the amplitude to encode the signal <span class="muted">(used in AM radio)</span>
- <span class="olive-bold">FM (Frequency Modulation)</span> — varies the frequency to encode the signal <span class="muted">(used in FM radio)</span>
- <span class="olive-bold">QAM (Quadrature Amplitude Modulation)</span> — varies both amplitude and phase <span class="muted">(used in Wi-Fi and cable)</span>

## Why It Matters

Modulation allows digital data to be transmitted over analog mediums like radio waves, telephone lines, and cable.

Without modulation, digital signals could not travel over most of the world's communication infrastructure.
