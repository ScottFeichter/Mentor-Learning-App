# Rules Of Statements

A statement consists of Lexicals, Expressions, or both.

---

## The Dividing Line

Ask one question: **is there an expression written in the syntax?**

- **Lexical Statement** — contains NO expressions. Lexicals only.
- **Expression Statement** — contains AT LEAST ONE expression. May also contain lexicals.

---

## Why This Works

It removes all ambiguity around mixed statements like `let x = 5`.

- `let x` — no expression written. Lexical Statement.
- `x = 5` — expression written. Expression Statement.
- `let x = 5` — expression written (`= 5`). Expression Statement — regardless of the `let` keyword also being present.

The presence of a lexical does not make a statement Lexical. The *absence* of any expression does.

---

## Lexical Statements Can Only Have Lexicals

A Lexical Statement contains keywords and/or punctuators only. The moment an operator or operand appears in the syntax, it becomes an Expression Statement.

## Expression Statements Can Have Lexicals Too

An Expression Statement may contain lexicals (keywords, punctuators) alongside expressions. The lexical does not change the category. The expression does.
