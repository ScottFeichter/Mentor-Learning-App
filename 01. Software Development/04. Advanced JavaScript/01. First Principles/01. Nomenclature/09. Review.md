# Review

&nbsp;

A summary of the key concepts covered in this topic.

---

## Semantic Drift

**Semantic drift** is when a word shifts away from its precise technical meaning through casual or inconsistent usage over time.

- happens in every field — not just programming
- especially common in programming because the same words get reused across different groups
- knowing precise terms helps you understand concepts clearly, read documentation accurately, and debug more effectively

---

## Terms Of Art

A **Term Of Art** is a word with a precise, agreed-upon meaning within a specific field.

- outside the field the word may mean something different or nothing at all
- using a **Term Of Art** loosely introduces ambiguity
- ambiguity causes miscommunication, misunderstanding, and bugs

---

## Jargon

**Jargon** is informal insider language — shorthand understood within a group but opaque to outsiders.

- useful for speed and efficiency among insiders
- becomes a problem when used with someone who doesn't know it yet
- becomes a problem when it replaces a term of art and loses precision

---

## Buzz Words

A **Buzz Word** is a term that spread quickly through marketing or hype and lost — or never had — a precise meaning.

- signals familiarity without communicating anything specific
- when a word means everything it means nothing
- ask: *what does this actually mean in concrete terms?* — if no one can answer, it is a **Buzz Word**

---

## JS Examples Of Semantic Drift

Terms in JS that have drifted from their precise meanings:

- `context` — used loosely to mean execution context or `this`
- `scope` — sometimes conflated with context
- `this` — confused with the English pronoun
- `binding` — used loosely to mean what `this` is bound to
- `hoisting` — described as variables "moving to the top"
- `variable` — used as a catch-all for any identifier
- `immutable` — `const` described as making values immutable, but it only fixes the binding
